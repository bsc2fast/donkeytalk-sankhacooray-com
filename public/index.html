The library that listens.

A Mac in the room hears what you hear. Whoever talks becomes a donkey on a big screen, voice by voice, with their words in a speech bubble, while a kind librarian asks for silence. Gentle shame, instant results.

How it works

Four steps, all on the Mac.

Put a Mac with a screen where people can see it. Start listening. That is the whole setup.

Prints become donkeysNew voice, new donkey. Same voice, same donkey. Four seats.
4
Silence redeemsTwo quiet minutes and the donkey leaves with honour.

Words in a bubble, live

Speech is transcribed on the Mac's GPU while the person is still talking. The bubble fills about a second after they start and is corrected when they stop.

The same voice, the same donkey

Voice prints are compared on the spot, so a repeat talker keeps their seat, their name card and a running word count. When the table is full, the nearest donkey takes the blame.

A librarian with opinions

She never speaks, but her caption does: dry remarks for noise, warm praise for silence, and a wall meter showing the room's decibels. Thirty quiet seconds earn a compliment.

Who it's for

Anyone who keeps a quiet room.

Built after too many evenings next to students who treat the reading room like a cafe. It scolds with humour, so people laugh and then lower their voice.

Librarians

Reading rooms and silent floors. The screen does the shushing, so you don't have to walk over every ten minutes.

Educators

Study halls, labs and exam prep rooms. A playful nudge lands better with students than another laminated sign.

Facilities and co-working

Quiet zones in shared offices, co-working spaces and hostels. Visible, fair and the same for everyone.

Curious makers

Researchers in HCI, speech or behaviour change who want to try the idea in their own space, or adapt the librarian to another setting.

Privacy

Loud in the room, silent on the network.

Everything runs on the Mac that is standing in the room. There is no account, no cloud service and no audio leaving the building.

On-deviceSpeech recognition and voice prints run on the Mac's own GPU. Switch the Wi-Fi off and it still works.
No recordingsAudio is processed as it arrives and discarded. Nothing is saved to disk, nothing is replayable.
No identitiesA voice print is a short list of numbers held in memory for the session. It is not linked to a name or a face and it is gone on reset.
Visible by designThe screen shows exactly what was heard. People see the system working; nothing happens behind their back.

What's next

A lamp over every table.

The screen names the donkey. The next step is to light up the table they are sitting at, so the whole room can see where the noise is coming from.

Philips Hue lamps, one per tablea small mic on each table measures loudness only
A mic on every table

Each table gets a small microphone. The Mac compares them and finds the loudest table in the room, the way a sound meter would, no words needed.

The lamp tells on you

Smart lamps over the tables glow soft amber while the room is quiet. The loudest table's lamp drifts to red for as long as the chatter lasts, then fades back as a reward for hushing.

Donkeys with a seat number

On the wall screen, the donkey appears above the table that is talking, so the librarian's remarks land on the right corner of the room.

Why a microphone is fair here. In a silent reading room nobody is supposed to be talking, so a table mic is a noise meter, not a listening device. The table lights only need loudness. Transcription stays optional and, as always, never leaves the Mac. It is social pressure with a wink: a little embarrassment, a lot of quiet.
